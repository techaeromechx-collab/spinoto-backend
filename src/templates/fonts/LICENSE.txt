The .woff2 files in this folder are SUBSETS of the upstream TrueType fonts
(Debian package `fonts-croscore`, version 20201225). They were reduced with
fontTools' pyftsubset to the character ranges an Indian GST invoice actually
prints — Basic Latin, Latin-1 Supplement, Latin Extended-A, common
typographic punctuation, and the Indian Rupee sign U+20B9 — then re-flavoured
to WOFF2. Subsetting and format conversion are expressly permitted by the
OFL; the fonts have not been renamed, and no Reserved Font Name applies.

Why these three specifically
────────────────────────────
They are metrically compatible, glyph for glyph, with the fonts the invoice
templates ask for:

    Arimo   ≡ Arial / Helvetica
    Tinos   ≡ Times New Roman  (the fallback behind Georgia)
    Cousine ≡ Courier New

"Metrically compatible" means every character occupies exactly the same
advance width as in the font it stands in for. Substituting them cannot
change where a line wraps or where a page breaks — which is the whole point
of shipping them rather than letting the host pick a substitute.

Do not replace these with a visually similar font that is not metrically
compatible (Liberation Sans is fine; Open Sans, Roboto and Noto Sans are
not). Doing so will silently reflow every invoice ever generated.
